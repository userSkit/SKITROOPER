On translations

The bit most reprints get wrong.

A book falling out of copyright does not make every version of it free. A translation is a new work with its own copyright, and the clock runs from the translator's death rather than the author's. Homer has been dead for nearly three thousand years. Robert Fagles died in 2008, and his Odyssey is protected until 2079.

So the cheap reprints you see on marketplaces are usually one of two things: an old translation nobody has bothered to set well, or a modern one nobody had permission to use. We do the first thing properly and never the second.

Homer here is Samuel Butler, 1898 and 1900, a prose translation you can read at the speed of a novel. Marcus Aurelius is George Long, 1862. Both translators died over a century ago and both texts are free and clear worldwide. Every title's page says which edition it came from and what year it dates to, because you should not have to guess.

Six of the ten were written in English and have no translator at all, which removes the question entirely.
